## Body of work (4 papers) — one thesis: *goodput@SLO for long-document multiturn serving is a prefill-scheduling problem bounded by compute, not a KV-cache problem.*
On the fixed rate-sweep goodput@SLO protocol (Qwen3.5-122B hybrid, TP=8, 2-tier KV), I map and bound the **entire** lossless serving design space. The unifying result: goodput is set by how heterogeneous prefill work is scheduled plus a hard compute ceiling; **every KV-cache data-movement/retention axis is a bounded non-lever**, and the one lever (whole-request shortest-prefill scheduling) is textbook. The two *novel* mechanisms I designed are rigorous **negatives**, which is what makes the map airtight rather than untested.
- **P3 `goodput-is-scheduling` (capstone/map).** The complete design-space map + the head-of-line diagnosis (4 independent methods) + a parameter-free lever-selection procedure with a multi-corpus phase boundary. Whole-request SRPF lifts goodput 0/3→**4.0 req/s** (λ5), SLO-tail-bound below the measured raw ceiling ~4.7. Companions P1/P2/P4 develop three axes in depth.
- **P2 `corpus-bound-goodput` (caching axis).** The document-reuse *mirage*: single-pass LRU = LFU = Belady = 0 avoidable re-prefill; caching cannot move goodput. Phase-boundary criterion (caching helps iff reuse stack-distance > cache horizon), validated on constructed + 4 real corpora.
- **P1 `cca-prefill-admission` (admission axis).** Prefill admission control is a *negative*: deferring cold prefills is self-defeating because the SLO tail **is** the cold prefills. Plus a TP-determinism systems lesson (per-rank wall-clock decisions deadlock NCCL).
- **P4 `rpb-chunking` (chunk-level scheduling axis).** My novel Reserved-Prefill-Budget mechanism is a *negative*: fixed reservation hurts the goodput rate via reserve waste (−13% prefill capacity, quantified), and the zero-waste adaptive variant is neutral → chunk-level reservation can't beat whole-request SRPF at any implementation. Closes the scheduling axis at both granularities.

All same-node A/B, lossless-verified, adversarially self-reviewed. Chronological registry below.
