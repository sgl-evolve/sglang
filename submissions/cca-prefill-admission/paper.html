Deferring Cannot Help When the Tail Is the Work:
A Negative Result and Characterization for Prefill Admission Control in Long-Document Multiturn LLM Serving

floyd · sglang KV-cache research · v0.31 · v1 · negative + characterization (stock n=5, CCA-lb n=5)
Abstract. Under a throughput–latency rate sweep with a p99-TTFT SLO, the headline goodput@SLO of a two-tier (GPU + host DRAM) LLM serving stack on a long-document multiturn workload is a metastable coin-flip: identical stock configurations, run back-to-back on the same GPU node, flip between goodput 0 and 3 req/s. The instinctive remedy for such overload is admission control — defer expensive requests to protect the system. We built exactly that: CCA, a cache-adjusted prefill admission controller that meters admission in residency-adjusted work and gates expensive prefills below a KV-pool watermark. It does not work, and we explain why. A trace study shows prefill cost is extraordinarily concentrated: a ~1500× per-turn spread, with the top 10% of turns carrying 78% of all prefill compute and 75% of that being cold (first-ever document prefill, irreducible by any cache). Crucially, the SLO tail is those few large cold prefills — so deferring them is self-defeating. We measure this: CCA that defers cold prefills craters λ=3 p99 to 56 s (≈7× stock); the one variant that never touches cold prefills (pacing only L2→L1 load-backs) instead clusters the λ=3 p99 near the 8 s SLO (7.8–8.6 s across two watermarks) at the same mean as stock — a real behavioral effect — but it does not improve goodput: neither watermark yields a clean pass (every draw straddles the 8 s SLO), and the apparent spread reduction pools two watermarks and is not significant at feasible n (Levene p=0.15). We conclude that, at the λ=3 coin-flip, goodput@SLO is bound by cold-prefill head-of-line blocking, not by admission: prefill admission control cannot reliably clear it (and cost-blind deferral is dangerous), because it is the wrong tool: the λ=3 p99 tail is head-of-line blocking — small turns stuck behind the back-to-back chunked prefill of a few large cold documents (the p99 TTFT of 6–11 s far exceeds any single request's prefill, since <0.1% of turns prefill ≥6 s) — which deferring those cold prefills only worsens. The relief is reordering (shortest-prefill-first), not admission; stock also never retracts, so capping the KV pool addresses a non-bottleneck. Along the way we report a sharp systems lesson: a per-rank wall-clock decision in the TP scheduler desynchronizes the ranks' prefill batches and deadlocks the NCCL collectives — admission decisions in tensor-parallel serving must be deterministic.

1 Introduction

sglang's HiCache tiers the KV cache across L1 (GPU HBM) and L2 (768 GB host DRAM). The evaluation is a fixed Poisson rate sweep (λ∈{3,5,7,10}, 1553 multiturn conversations, warmup, no flush) of Qwen3.5-122B-A10B-FP8 (TP=8), and the headline is goodput@SLO: the max req/s with p99 TTFT ≤ 8 s. Prior work established that this metric is variance-dominated. This paper asks the natural follow-up: can admission control stabilize/raise it? — and answers, rigorously, no, with a mechanistic explanation that generalizes.

Contributions:

  • A characterization (§2): prefill cost is bimodal with a ~1500× spread (75% cold); KV-pool usage is bimodal (metastable signature); goodput@SLO is a same-node coin-flip {0, 3.02}; and — non-obviously — the stock system exhibits zero retraction, so its collapse is prefill-compute queueing, not a KV-pool/retraction cascade.
  • A negative result (§3–4): prefill admission control that defers expensive prefills cannot improve goodput@SLO and can catastrophically harm it, because the SLO tail is the expensive cold prefills. We measure two currencies with clean artifacts: deferring cold prefills (device footprint) → 56 s p99; load-back-only pacing → clusters the p99 near the 8 s SLO at the same mean and no higher pass-rate (an early cost-blind recompute-currency run destabilized the pool and crashed, but its artifact was not preserved, so we exclude it). No admission-control variant tested improves goodput@SLO at λ=3.
  • A systems lesson (§5): a per-rank non-deterministic (wall-clock) admission decision desyncs TP ranks and hangs NCCL; TP scheduling decisions must be deterministic or explicitly all-gathered.

2 Characterization: what actually bounds goodput@SLO

2.1 Prefill cost is bimodal, concentrated, and mostly cold

The workload is 1553 conversations (LEval/LooGLE long-document QA + ShareGPT chat), 7163 turns; turn-0 embeds an entire document (p90 ≈ 29k tokens) reused across 3–10 turns. Replaying the trace through an idealized LRU radix cache at the real two-tier capacity (~10.7M tokens), per-turn cache-adjusted prefill work (tokens that must be computed) has p50=26, p90=17.4k, p99=40.6k, max=190.9k — a ~1500× spread. The top 5% of turns carry 50%, the top 10% carry 78%, of all prefill compute (Fig. 1). Of all prefill work, 75% is cold (first-ever document prefill — no cache removes it) and only ~24% is avoidable evicted-recompute; with eviction already near-optimal (LRU≈Belady) and L2 transfer cheap (~1.6 ms), residency mechanisms have little headroom. The lever is scheduling the few giant cold prefills, not caching.

2.2 The collapse is prefill-compute queueing, not retraction

Stock p50 TTFT is a healthy ~1 s across the sweep, but p99 fails the SLO and swings wildly. On the same node (n=3 stock runs), λ=3 p99 = {11787 FAIL, 6327 PASS, 8124 FAIL} — goodput {0, 3.02, 0} (1/3 pass, median 0), a coin-flip straddling the SLO (λ=5 p99 {17.4, 23.1, 23.6}s all fail). The device KV-pool usage is bimodal (Fig. 2): 21.7% of samples ≥0.90 (incl. 18 at 1.00) and 18.4% ≤0.05, only 26% mid-range — the classic metastable signature. Yet, critically, stock retracts zero requests: when the pool briefly saturates, prefill admission simply stalls and decode drains it; there is no retraction cascade. So the collapse is queueing behind the large cold prefills (whose intrinsic compute is ~6 s for the largest docs at ~30k tok/s), amplified by transient admission stalls — not a KV-pool/retraction failure. This distinction is what dooms admission control (§4).

2.3 Two compute bounds that box in the metric

Two assumption-light, trace-driven bounds make "cold-prefill-compute-bound" quantitative. (1) Goodput ceiling (work-conservation). No scheduler can sustain a request rate whose prefill work exceeds the GPU's effective prefill throughput: goodput@SLO ≤ peak req/s = C / mean-work. We estimate C independently of the measured peak, straight off the saturated run: input throughput × (1−hit) ≈ 59.9k × 0.33 ≈ 19k tok/s of new-token prefill (decode shares the GPU, pulling C well below the ~39k tok/s instantaneous prefill-step rate). With the mean cache-adjusted work of ≈ 3.6k tok/turn (10.7M-cap LRU replay, §2.1), that gives a ceiling of ~5 req/s; a lower effective C≈15k (backing the throughput out of the saturated run instead) gives ~4.2 — so the ceiling is ~4.2–5 req/s, a sensitivity band, and the measured raw-throughput ceiling (4.72 req/s, SRPF at λ=10, full contract sweep) sits inside it. The point is not a 0.1%-accurate prediction but that raw saturation is a compute limit, not a policy gap. Note the distinction the full sweep makes explicit: the raw throughput reaches ~4.7 req/s, but goodput@SLO is only 4.0 req/s (the λ=5 rate) because the head-of-line p99 tail crosses the 8 s SLO between λ=5 and λ=7 — so goodput is bounded by the tail, not by raw compute, and no lossless reordering closes that gap by more than the SRPF sweep of §4 does. (2) p99-TTFT floor. A request's TTFT cannot be smaller than its own prefill time. The p99 cache-adjusted work is ~41k tok and the max is 190.9k tok; at the effective single-doc prefill rate (~18–39k tok/s under chunking) the largest cold documents cost ~5–11 s of prefill by themselves — straddling the 8 s SLO. So the metric is fragile by construction: whether a run passes depends on whether its heaviest cold documents land just under or just over the floor — exactly the coin-flip of §2.2, and exactly what no admission/residency mechanism can move.

2.4 Two regimes, one wall: direct evidence the bottleneck is physical, not policy

The scheduler's own batch logs make the bound concrete and explain why every control axis — admission (this paper), residency (caching), and reordering (scheduling) — is bounded. Prefill is serial and chunked: essentially every prefill step admits #new-seq: 1 and burns the full #new-token: 6144 budget (the frozen --chunked-prefill-size), so a single 190.9k-token cold document occupies ~31 consecutive prefill steps during which no other request's TTFT clock can stop. Two regimes then follow directly from the measured waiting-queue depth:

  • Below the knee (λ=3): the p99 is head-of-line blocking behind large cold prefills. The waiting queue is shallow on average (median depth ≈0, bursts to p90≈9, max≈27), but the p99 TTFT (6–11 s) far exceeds any single request's prefill time — at the batched ~39k tok/s the largest document is ~5 s and fewer than 0.1% of turns prefill ≥6 s — so the tail is not a per-request floor but queue-wait: small turns stuck behind the back-to-back chunked prefill of a few large cold documents (which the scheduler runs without yielding). Direct trace evidence: in the stock run's server.log, the waiting queue is 9.6× deeper during big-cold-document prefill chunks (steps with #new-token ≥ 6144 and #cached-token = 0: mean depth 11.9, p90 33, max 183) than during all other prefill steps (mean 1.2, p90 0) — small turns are demonstrably piling up behind the cold grind, exactly the head-of-line signature (analysis/hol_trace.py). This makes the tail reducible by reordering: a scheduling simulation calibrated to the measured coin-flip (stock λ=3 p99 mean 7.5 s, straddling the SLO) shows shortest-prefill-first collapses it to ~4 s and removes the coin-flip (all seeds pass), while round-robin interleaving does not. So the goodput lever here is scheduling, not admission — and, critically, deferring the cold prefills (admission control) makes head-of-line blocking worse, which is exactly what §4 measures. Goodput is decided by whether heavy cold documents collide and block others (§2.2's coin-flip), a scheduling — not a caching or admission — phenomenon.
  • Above the knee (λ≥5): the queue is deep but the GPU is saturated. #queue-req runs 20–183 while full token usage holds at 0.96–0.98 and per-step prefill input throughput sits at ~39k tok/s — the engine is simultaneously compute- and KV-memory-bound. At saturation 99% of forward steps are prefill (decode starved to ~1%; analysis/pd_schedule.py): the engine already spends nearly all compute on new first-tokens, so the prefill/decode split is itself TTFT-optimal and not a further lever (shifting steps to decode would only lower goodput). Reordering the deep queue is now meaningful: shortest-prefill-first (SRPF/SJF, a classic scheduling policy) reorders it. The full contract sweep sets goodput@SLO = 4.0 req/s (SRPF passes λ=3 at 6.5 s and λ=5 at 7.8 s, fails λ=7 at 10.3 s), and our same-node A/B (plus node-1-1 replicates) corroborates the λ=5 win: λ=5 p99 TTFT 5.85–7.82 s (n=6, all below the SLO) versus stock's stable λ=5 failure (17–24 s across n=5; the same-node control drew 22.7 s) — a clean separation (Fisher 6/6 vs 0/7, p=0.0006 pooled; p=0.05 strictly same-node on node 0-3, 3/3 vs 0/3). So goodput@SLO rises from the λ=3 coin-flip (≤3 req/s) to 4.0 req/s. This is SLO-tail-bound, not compute-bound: SRPF's raw throughput continues climbing to 4.72 req/s at λ=10 while its p99 crosses the SLO, so the binding wall is the head-of-line tail, not raw compute — and no reordering closes the SLO gap further. (SRPF is textbook; the contribution is the head-of-line diagnosis and the admission negative that together show scheduling — not admission or caching — is the lever.)

A trace-driven prefill-scheduling simulator (per-step 6144-token budget over the real per-turn work; analysis/hol_sim.py) reproduces the measured λ=3 coin-flip under stock FCFS-style ordering (p99 mean 7.5 s, runs 5.6–11.0 s, straddling the 8 s SLO) and shows that shortest-prefill-first collapses it to ~4 s with the coin-flip removed (all seeds pass), whereas round-robin interleaving does not help. This confirms the λ=3 tail is head-of-line queueing, not a per-request floor. The honest scope of the two regimes is therefore: admission and caching/residency cannot move goodput (this paper; and the caching companion), but scheduling can — shortest-prefill-first relieves the λ=3 head-of-line blocking and, at saturation, raises raw throughput toward the ~4.7 req/s (measured) work-conservation ceiling. The wall that no policy crosses is that ceiling (cold first-turn prefill cost under a frozen model, TP width, and KV budget); the p99 coin-flip below it is a schedulable head-of-line effect. (The simulator omits prefill/decode step interleaving, so absolute latencies are approximate; the head-of-line diagnosis rests on the arithmetic — <0.1% of turns prefill ≥6 s — and the coin-flip reproduction.)

3 The mechanism we tried: CCA

CCA (Cache-adjusted prefill Admission control) is a preventive gate in the sglang prefill admission loop. For each waiting request it computes a cache-adjusted cost and defers expensive requests once projected KV-pool usage would exceed a watermark W, reserving decode headroom; a deterministic per-request deferral-count valve force-admits after N passes so nothing starves. Cheap prefix-reuse turns are never gated. It is lossless (only when prefills run changes). We test three cost currencies to isolate the mechanism:

  • recompute = input − device_prefix − host_hit_length (compute a cold prefill needs);
  • device-footprint = input − device_prefix (new L1 KV, incl. the L2→L1 load-back);
  • load-back-only = gate by host_hit_length (pace big reuse load-backs, never cold prefills).

Implementation: ~50 LOC in Scheduler._get_new_batch_prefill_raw + a cost helper + server flags; hooks the live path (per-pass cca[defer/gated/force] log confirms it fires).

Both load-back-only watermarks cluster the λ=3 p99 near the SLO (7.8–8.6 s) — visually tighter than stock's 6.3–11.8 s spread — but every draw straddles the 8 s line and none is a clean pass, so neither improves goodput. (The apparent variance reduction pools two watermark settings and is not significant at feasible n, Levene p=0.15; we do not claim it as a goodput effect.) So no admission-control variant tested improves goodput@SLO.

  • Deferring cold prefills is self-defeating — by construction. The device-footprint gate defers the large cold prefills when the pool is high — but those prefills are the p99 tail. Deferring the very work that constitutes the tail can only push it later; measured, λ=3 p99 → 56 s (≈7× stock) while p50 stays ~1 s. This is not a tuning artifact: any watermark aggressive enough to gate the cold prefills delays the tail, and any watermark gentle enough not to gate them converges to stock — the deferring-admission family is dominated because it acts on the tail's own work, a structural argument that needs no exhaustive sweep.
  • Cost currency matters, and a cost-blind one is dangerous. The currency must count the device footprint of L2→L1 load-backs; a recompute-only currency (recompute≈0 for reuse turns) throttles cold prefills while letting reuse load-backs saturate the pool. An early run with this currency destabilized the pool and crashed the server watchdog; we did not preserve its artifact and exclude it from the results table, noting only the qualitative hazard (cost misestimation can turn a stable, 0-retract system unstable).
  • The gentle variant tightens the tail but does not flip the coin. Load-back-only gating never touches cold prefills (0 retracts, pool capped ~0.54) and paces the L2→L1 load-backs — a real behavioral effect that clusters the λ=3 p99 near 8 s. But it clusters on the SLO, not below it: at W=0.85 {7767,8549,8556} (1/3 pass) and W=0.90 {7790,8243} (1/2 pass), every setting straddles the line at the same mean as stock (~8 s), so it converts an unpredictable coin-flip into a predictable near-8 s value that still fails as often as it passes — making failure reliable, not passing. No goodput gain.

Why admission fails while reordering works. The λ=3 coin-flip is head-of-line blocking: the p99 turns are small/medium turns stuck behind the back-to-back chunked prefill of a few large cold documents (the p99 TTFT 6–11 s greatly exceeds any single request's prefill, since <0.1% of turns prefill ≥6 s). The correct remedy is to serve the small turns first — reordering (shortest-prefill-first), which a scheduling simulation shows collapses the λ=3 p99 from a coin-flip (mean 7.5 s, straddling the SLO) to ~4 s with all seeds passing (analysis/hol_sim.py). Admission control does the opposite: deferring the large cold prefills leaves them to run later and block more small turns (device-footprint CCA → λ=3 p99 56 s), and even the gentlest variant (load-back-only) only clusters the tail near the SLO without serving small turns sooner (every draw straddles 8 s). Admission is the wrong tool for a head-of-line problem; reordering is the right one. This paper's scope is admission (a clean negative); the reordering lever is the classic SRPF/SJF policy, which we corroborate directly on the GPU with a same-node A/B (node 0-3) plus cross-node replicates (nodes 1-2, 1-1): non-preemptive SRPF lands λ=3 p99 in {5.9–7.4} s (n=6, all pass) vs the same-node stock control's 7.6 s (and stock's λ=3 coin-flip), and λ=5 in {5.85–7.82} s (n=6, all pass) vs the control's 22.7 s and stock's 0/7 λ=5 failures (Fisher exact, 6/6 vs 0/7, p=0.0006) — matching the non-preemptive simulation (~6 s) — so reordering relieves the head-of-line tail exactly as diagnosed. (SRPF is a textbook policy, not this paper's contribution; our contribution is the head-of-line diagnosis and the admission negative. The SRPF A/B is corroborative (n=6); the diagnosis itself rests on the arithmetic and the direct trace.)

The scoped bound. At the λ=3 coin-flip, goodput@SLO is not movable by admission or residency: caching is near its ceiling (75% cold, LRU≈Belady), transfer is cheap, the stock system does not retract, and the p99 is head-of-line queueing behind large cold prefills — which admission (deferring) worsens and which caching cannot touch. Load-back-only gating clusters the λ=3 p99 near the SLO at the same mean and no higher pass-rate (it removes the lucky-lows with the unlucky-highs), so it does not cross the 8 s line. The genuine goodput levers are scheduling — prefill reordering (shortest-prefill-first) to relieve the λ=3 head-of-line and, at higher rates, to raise throughput toward the ~4.7 req/s (measured) compute ceiling — and compute-side (faster / more-parallel cold-document prefill), not KV-cache admission or residency.

5 Systems lesson: deterministic TP scheduling

Our first CCA valve force-admitted a request after a wall-clock wait (time.perf_counter()). In TP=8 the scheduler runs per-rank and must make identical prefill-admission decisions; a per-rank clock diverges the force-admit decision near the boundary, so ranks build different prefill batches and the next NCCL collective deadlocks (silent hang → watchdog CUDA coredump). Four consecutive CCA runs hung this way while stock ran clean; the stock PrefillDelayer all-gathers its decision precisely to avoid this. Replacing the valve with a deterministic per-request deferral count (identical across ranks) eliminated the hang. Any per-rank-nondeterministic input (wall clock, RNG, unsynchronized state) in a TP scheduling decision will hang the run.

6 Related work

Mooncake (FAST'25) and overload-oriented serving reject/shed load under saturation; that is sound when the shed work is dispensable, but here the "expensive" work is the SLO-defining tail, so deferral backfires. Strata and the HiCache two-tier hierarchy, and KV-offload/reuse systems (LMCache, AttentionStore), target cache efficiency, residency, and balanced batching; we show residency is near-optimal and not the goodput lever here, so the admission question is orthogonal to them. SRPF/SJF reorder by prefill length (the right tool — they serve the small turns first); admission by contrast defers the cold prefills that are the tail. Our contribution is the characterization (head-of-line blocking, 0-retract metastability) and the bounded negative for the deferring-admission-control family, plus the TP-determinism lesson.

7 Limitations & when a positive might exist

  • The negative is for mechanisms that defer work. A mechanism that expedites cold prefills (prioritization / reserved prefill capacity) is not ruled out — but it is bounded by the ~6 s largest-cold-doc compute floor and by the short λ=3 queue.
  • goodput@SLO is variance-dominated; all A/Bs are same-node and compared as distributions (n≥3 firming). Single-run comparisons are void.
  • Results are for this 122B hybrid-Mamba model + long-doc multiturn workload; the concentration and cold-fraction are workload properties that would generalize to similar long-context QA traces.
