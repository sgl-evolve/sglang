Cache-Adjusted Prefill Admission Control:
Keeping Goodput Off the Retraction Cliff in Two-Tier LLM Serving

floyd · sglang KV-cache research · v0.31 · draft (evaluation in progress)
Abstract. Long-context multiturn LLM serving over a two-tier (GPU HBM + host DRAM) KV cache exhibits a metastable tail-latency collapse: identical stock configurations flip between passing and failing a p99 TTFT SLO run-to-run, so goodput@SLO behaves like a coin toss. We trace this instability to a cost-blind admission policy. A trace-driven analysis of the fixed evaluation workload shows prefill cost is extraordinarily heterogeneous — cache-adjusted per-turn work spans ~1500× (median 26 tokens of prefix-reuse vs. p99 of 40.6k tokens of cold document), and the top 5% of turns carry 50%, the top 10% carry 78%, of all prefill compute. The scheduler admits prefill by raw token budget and request count, so a burst of a few cold-document prefills — a handful of requests but the bulk of the work — saturates the KV pool (we observe stock reaching 100% device-pool usage repeatedly), stalling admission and spiking the TTFT tail. We introduce Cache-Adjusted prefill admission control (CCA): a preventive gate that meters admission in cache-adjusted work (input minus radix-resident prefix) and defers only expensive prefills once projected pool usage would breach a watermark, reserving decode headroom to keep the system off the retraction cliff. Cheap prefix-reuse turns are never gated; a starvation valve bounds worst-case TTFT. CCA is lossless — it changes only when prefills run, never KV contents or outputs. [Evaluation in progress: same-node replicated rate-sweep A/B, goodput@SLO, ablations. Preliminary: CCA caps device-pool usage at 0.70 vs. stock's repeated 1.00.]

1 Introduction

sglang's HiCache tiers the KV cache across L1 (GPU HBM) and L2 (768 GB host DRAM). Under an open-loop Poisson rate sweep of a realistic long-document multiturn workload, the headline metric is goodput@SLO: the maximum request rate sustaining p99 TTFT ≤ 8 s. Prior measurement on this platform established that goodput@SLO is variance-dominated — a metastable coin-flip in which the same stock build passes or fails at random. That work characterized the instability but did not cure it.

This paper makes three contributions:

  • A diagnosis. The collapse is a control instability driven by cost misestimation. Prefill cost varies ~1500× across turns because of cache residency, yet admission is metered in request count / raw tokens, so a small cluster of cold-document prefills tips the KV pool into saturation and stalls the pipeline.
  • A mechanism. CCA — work-bounded prefill admission in cache-adjusted currency. The right unit for admission control is residency-adjusted compute work, not requests or raw tokens. To our knowledge no serving system meters admission this way.
  • Evidence. [rate-sweep curve vs. baseline; goodput@SLO; ablations isolating the cache-adjustment; n≥2 same-node replicates; lossless verification. In progress.]

2 Motivation

2.1 The workload: a few giant cold prefills amid a sea of cheap reuse

The fixed workload is 1553 multiturn conversations (LEval/LooGLE long-document QA + ShareGPT chat) replayed at Poisson rate, 7163 total turns. Turn 0 embeds an entire document (p90 ≈29k tokens); turns 1..N reuse it through the radix cache. We replay the trace through an idealized LRU radix cache at the real two-tier capacity (~10.7M tokens) and measure each turn's cache-adjusted prefill work = tokens that must actually be computed (input − resident prefix).

Two facts follow. First, caching is near its ceiling here: of all prefill work, 75% is cold (first-ever document prefill — unavoidable by any cache) and only ~24% is avoidable evicted-recompute; with eviction already near-optimal (LRU≈Belady) and L2 transfer cheap (~1.6 ms), residency mechanisms have little headroom left. Second, the lever is scheduling, not caching: cost is so concentrated that how the few giant prefills are admitted dominates the tail.

2.2 The collapse: cost-blind admission saturates the pool

goodput@SLO is a p99 metric. In our stock baseline the whole rate sweep FAILS the 8 s SLO while p50 stays healthy (~1 s): λ=3 p99=11.8 s, λ=5 17.4 s, λ=7 34.0 s (peak ~4 req/s, ~512 tok/s). The tail is not steady-state; it is a few events. The device KV pool usage is strikingly bimodal (Figure 2): 21.7% of the time it is near-saturated (≥0.90, incl. 18 events at exactly 1.00) and 18.4% idle (≤0.05), with only 26% mid-range. The pool oscillates between empty and full — the classic signature of a metastable queue. At saturation, prefill admission stalls (no free tokens), queued requests wait, and the TTFT tail explodes. Because admission is metered by request count and raw tokens, the scheduler cannot see that three "requests" are actually 120k tokens of compute about to saturate the pool. That misestimation is the instability.

3 Design

3.1 Mechanism

CCA adds a preventive gate to the prefill admission loop. For each waiting request it computes the cache-adjusted cost 

cost(req) = len(fill_ids) − len(device_prefix) − host_hit_length     # tokens to actually compute

i.e. total tokens minus what the radix cache already holds (device prefix + host prefix that loads back cheaply). A prefix-reuse turn is ~0; a cold document is tens of thousands. The gate: 

if cost > θ                                  # expensive (cold) prefill only
   and running_batch nonempty                # progress guarantee: never stall an empty pipeline
   and base_used + admitted + cost > W·pool: # projected pool usage breaches watermark
      if req.waited < max_defer:  DEFER      # keep it queued; reserve decode headroom
      else:                        ADMIT      # starvation valve bounds worst-case TTFT

base_used is the pool usage snapshotted this pass; admitted accumulates the cost of prefills already admitted this pass, so the projection sees the batch being built. Cheap turns (cost≤θ) are never gated — the 70% near-free reuse stream flows unimpeded. Deferred requests stay in the waiting queue and are reconsidered next pass, once decode drains the pool below the watermark. The valve force-admits any expensive prefill that has already waited max_defer, bounding its TTFT under the SLO.

3.2 Why it is novel

  • vs. SRPF/SJF: those reorder the queue; they do not bound in-flight work, so a burst still saturates the pool. Here reordering is counterproductive — the cold prefills that SRPF defers are the p99, so deferring them raises the tail.
  • vs. the stock PrefillDelayer: that batches prefills to avoid decode-batch fragmentation, keyed on slot/queue counts and current pool usage; it is cache-blind and off by default. CCA meters cache-adjusted work and is predictive of the retraction cliff.
  • vs. reactive retraction (check_decode_mem): stock admits greedily until the pool is full, then retracts running requests (re-prefill — pure waste). CCA is the preventive dual: don't admit what would force a retraction.
  • The insight: prefill cost varies 1500× because of cache residency, so admission must be metered in residency-adjusted work to stay off the cliff. This generalizes to any prefix-cached long-context serving system.

4 Implementation

CCA is ~40 lines in the sglang scheduler, hooking the live prefill admission path (Scheduler._get_new_batch_prefill_raw), plus a cost helper and four server-args knobs (--enable-cca-prefill, --cca-watermark, --cca-threshold, --cca-max-defer-ms, and --cca-use-raw-cost for the ablation). It touches no KV data, no prefix matching, no token generation — proving losslessness by construction. A per-pass log (cca[defer/gated-pass/force]) confirms the gate exercises the live path. Commit 8ca2a0ea4 on evolve/floyd.

5 Evaluation

In progress. Protocol: fixed rate sweep λ∈{3,5,7,10}, NUMP=1553, warmup burst, no flush; headline goodput@SLO (p99 TTFT≤8 s); honest controls peak tok/s & req/s (decode-bound, expected flat). Plan: (a) same-node stock-vs-CCA A/B, n≥2 replicates each (goodput@SLO is variance-dominated → compare p99 distributions, Levene + Mann-Whitney, not single runs); (b) ablations — --cca-use-raw-cost (remove cache-adjustment), stock PrefillDelayer, watermark/threshold sweep; (c) lossless verification via greedy-decode output diff.

Preliminary mechanism result (node-independent). With CCA enabled, device-pool usage is capped at 0.70 with 0 saturation events, versus stock reaching 1.00 ten times at λ=3; CCA sustains 70–99 concurrently-running requests at 0.20–0.36 pool usage while the gate fires (thousands of deferrals, hundreds of valve force-admits). The mechanism does what it is designed to do — the open question the full curve answers is whether pool control translates into a p99/goodput win.

6 Related work

Mooncake (FAST'25) disaggregates prefill/decode and rejects load on overload; Strata (arXiv 2508.18572) balances prefill/decode batching for cache efficiency; the sglang HiCache blog introduces the two-tier hierarchy. All admit prefill by token/slot budgets. CCA differs by metering admission in cache-adjusted compute work and targeting the metastable pool-saturation collapse specifically. Classic admission control / load shedding and CPU cache hierarchies inform the framing; the novelty is the residency-adjusted currency and the preventive-retraction target.

7 Limitations & when it fails

  • Under sustained saturation (λ far past the knee) no admission policy can help — the valve degrades CCA gracefully to stock. CCA targets the metastable knee, not hard overload.
  • The cost currency counts recompute, not the device footprint of large L2→L1 load-backs; a burst of reuse turns loading big prefixes could still pressure the pool. A device-footprint currency variant is a one-line change under evaluation.
  • The p99 floor is the intrinsic compute time of the largest cold document (~6 s at ~30k tok/s); CCA cannot beat that floor, only prevent queueing on top of it.
