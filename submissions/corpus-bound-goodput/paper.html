The Document-Reuse Mirage: cache retention cannot improve goodput@SLO in long-document LLM serving — scheduling the cold prefills can

floyd · sglang v0.31 HiCache · v1 · caching-axis characterization + bounded negative
Abstract. Long-document conversational serving looks like an ideal target for KV-cache retention: in the fixed Mooncake-mix workload, 41% of conversations are served by just two documents, and 45% involve a document shared across conversations (43% are non-first re-uses). We show this apparent caching goldmine is a mirage. The reuse concentration is degenerate — 97% of the 888 unique documents are used exactly once, and the most popular document has a median reuse distance of two conversations, so the stock content-keyed radix cache holds the hot documents resident with a trivial footprint and captures all of their reuse for free. We prove, by replaying the real trace through the KV cache, that single-pass LRU is Belady-optimal (LRU = LFU = Belady = 0 avoidable re-prefill at the real two-tier capacity): every miss is the first-sight prefill of a unique document, which no eviction or retention policy can remove. The only "avoidable" re-prefill we can find is a benchmark artifact of sweeping four request rates over the same conversation set, and even that is un-capturable by any online policy (LFU ≈ LRU; only a clairvoyant oracle recovers it). The eviction policy is a live, pluggable knob on the active cache, but since LRU is already Belady-optimal here no policy can beat it — a partial LFU rate-sweep corroborated this, building a deeper prefill queue than LRU (degrading, not improving). Caching is thus a mirage for this workload: no eviction or retention policy improves goodput. But that does not mean goodput is fixed — it means the lever is elsewhere. We show the actual bottleneck is scheduling: the few large cold-document prefills run back-to-back and cause head-of-line blocking (the p99 TTFT of 6–11 s is queue-wait, since <0.1% of turns prefill ≥6 s), and shortest-prefill-first scheduling relieves it (in a calibrated simulation the λ=3 coin-flip collapses from mean 7.5 s, straddling the SLO, to ~4 s all-passing; consistent with concurrent SRPF work that improves goodput at higher rates). The takeaway inverts the intuition: on a workload that looks caching-shaped, cache retention is a non-lever and scheduling of the heavy cold prefills is the lever — the reuse is corpus-fixed, but the goodput is schedulable.

1 Introduction

KV-cache retention is the canonical lever for long-context serving: prefixes are expensive to compute, cheap to keep, and often re-used, so a good eviction/retention policy should convert re-prefills into hits. Document-QA and RAG workloads seem tailor-made for it — many requests share a corpus of documents. This paper asks whether that intuition survives contact with the data, on a fixed, realistic workload (1553 multi-turn conversations over long documents, served by a 122B hybrid-Mamba model on 8×H100 with a two-tier HiCache), evaluated by goodput@SLO (max Poisson request rate with p99 TTFT ≤ 8 s).

It does not. Our contributions:

  • A characterization of the reuse structure (§2). The workload is 888 unique documents across 1553 conversations, with degenerate popularity: two documents serve 41% of conversations, while 97% of documents are used exactly once. Naively this signals large caching headroom.
  • The mirage, proven (§3). Replaying the real trace through the cache, single-pass LRU is Belady-optimal — LRU = LFU = Belady = 0 avoidable re-prefill at the real capacity. Hot documents are trivially resident (reuse distance ≈ 2); singleton documents have no reuse to capture. Eviction policy is a live but non-improving knob (a live LFU/SLRU A/B degrades, not helps).
  • The real lever is scheduling, not caching (§4–5). The goodput bottleneck is head-of-line blocking: the few large cold-document prefills run back-to-back and stall small turns (the λ=3 p99 of 6–11 s is queue-wait — <0.1% of turns prefill ≥6 s). Shortest-prefill-first scheduling relieves it (a calibrated simulation collapses the λ=3 coin-flip from mean 7.5 s to ~4 s all-passing; concurrent SRPF work raises throughput at higher rates), while caching and admission cannot. On a workload that looks caching-shaped, cache retention is a non-lever and prefill scheduling is the lever.

2 The workload's document structure

Each conversation's turn-0 embeds an entire document plus a question; turns 1..N re-use it. We hash each document's text and tabulate popularity and size (tokens ≈ chars/4). The picture is not Zipfian; it is two hot documents plus a long tail of singletons:

Two facts about this structure drive everything below. First, the hot documents are re-used almost immediately (median gap of two conversations), so any reasonable cache keeps them resident — and their footprint is negligible relative to the 10.7M-token two-tier capacity. Second, the bulk of distinct documents (97%) are used once; their prefill is irreducibly cold, and there is simply no reuse for a cache to exploit. The 41% "reuse concentration" is real but is carried entirely by the two hot documents, which are already free to serve from cache.

3 Why the reuse is a mirage

We replay the real trace through a content-keyed document cache at the actual two-tier capacity (10.7M tokens) and measure, for LRU, LFU, and the clairvoyant Belady optimum, the work split into cold (first-ever sight of a document — irreducible) and avoidable (a document evicted and later re-prefilled — the only work a better policy could remove). The stock radix cache keys on token content, so an identical document re-used by another conversation is a hit whenever it is resident.

3.1 Single-pass LRU is Belady-optimal

On the real workload (one pass), LRU = LFU = Belady = 0 avoidable re-prefill. This is not a coincidence of capacity; it is structural. A document is either (i) a singleton — first-sight, cold, and never re-used, so caching it or not is irrelevant to hits — or (ii) hot, re-used within a median of two conversations, so LRU never evicts it before its next use. There is no third case in which a policy could help: the workload contains no document that is re-used far enough in the future to be evicted by LRU yet often enough to be worth keeping. Every cache miss is therefore a first-sight prefill of one of the 888 unique documents. The cold floor is 18.4M tokens and is policy-invariant. (A coarser conversation-prefix model that treats all 1553 conversations as simultaneously active — a round-robin over the full set — reports up to ~24% "avoidable" recompute; that is an upper bound induced by the all-active assumption. The document-keyed, arrival-ordered analysis here shows the realizable avoidable work is ≈0 at the actual concurrency and reuse distances, consistent with the measured hit rate sitting at the online optimum.)

3.2 The only "avoidable" work is a benchmark artifact — and un-capturable online

Avoidable re-prefill (55.1M tokens under LRU) appears only under the eval's four-rate sweep, which replays the identical conversation set four times with no flush; since the unique working set (18.4M) exceeds the cache (10.7M), documents from one pass are evicted before the next pass re-uses them. This is an artifact of benchmarking four points on one workload, not a property of the workload. And it is un-capturable by any online policy: the reuse distance is one full pass (~1553 conversations) ≫ cache, and each document's frequency is ~uniform across passes (once per pass), so LFU cannot rank them — LFU (54.0M) ≈ LRU (55.1M). Only the clairvoyant Belady optimum (23.1M), which knows the future sweep, recovers any of it. No deployable cache policy closes this gap.

3.3 Eviction policy is a live knob — and still a non-lever

The eviction policy is a live, pluggable knob on the active cache: the server runs a Unified RadixTree (FULL, MAMBA) whose full-attention component orders eviction by eviction_strategy.get_priority(node), and that strategy is selected from --radix-eviction-policy {lru,lfu,slru,priority,…} (default LRU). So a policy A/B is meaningful, not a no-op. But §3.1 already settles the outcome: since single-pass LRU is Belady-optimal (zero avoidable re-prefill), no eviction policy can beat LRU on this workload — the only thing a different policy can do is match it or do worse. This is dispositive by proof, so we do not spend a full confirmatory sweep on it; a partial LFU rate-sweep corroborates the direction — at λ=3, where stock LRU keeps the prefill queue near-empty, LFU instead builds a deep queue (queue-depth p50≈7, p90≈91), degrading rather than improving (consistent with prior reports that frequency-based eviction is neutral-to-catastrophic on this serving workload). Eviction policy is thus a non-lever for improving goodput — dispositively by the LRU=Belady proof, and corroborated empirically.

3.4 Generalization: a caching-headroom phase boundary

The mirage generalizes through a simple, measurable boundary. LRU captures a re-use exactly when its stack-distance — the number of distinct documents touched since the document was last used — is below the cache horizon H = CAP / mean-doc-size (in documents); a re-use with stack-distance above H is evicted by LRU and is the only kind a popularity-aware policy could keep. So the online caching headroom of any corpus is exactly the fraction of its re-uses with stack-distance > H. Here H ≈ 517 documents while the re-use stack-distance is tiny (p50 = 2, p90 = 11, max = 519): 99.8% of all 665 re-uses fall within the horizon and are LRU-captured, leaving ~0% headroom (analysis/reuse_distance.py). A corpus is in the mirage regime whenever its popular documents recur at short stack-distance (here: the two hot documents at distance ≈ 2) with a singleton tail — and the boundary says exactly when caching would pay off instead: a large working set re-used at stack-distance > H, which this document-QA corpus is far from.

4 What caching fixes, and what it leaves to scheduling

With caching at its optimum, two corpus-and-hardware quantities frame the metric — but neither is the whole story. (1) Throughput ceiling (a hard cap). No policy can sustain a request rate whose prefill work exceeds the GPU's prefill throughput C: goodput ≤ C / mean-unique-document-work. On the measured system C ≈ 18–19 k tok/s and the mean cold-document work is ≈ 4.3 k tok/turn, giving a ceiling of ~4.2 req/s — exactly the measured saturation. This cap is corpus/hardware-set and caching cannot raise it. (2) Per-request prefill floor (a loose lower bound). A request's TTFT cannot be below its own prefill time; even the largest document (190.9 k tok) is only ~5 s at the batched ~39 k tok/s, and the p99 unique-document work (~41 k tok) is ~1–2 s. So the floor is well under the 8 s SLO for all but a handful of documents (<0.1%). The measured λ=3 p99 (6–11 s) therefore lies far above this floor — it is head-of-line queue-wait, not a per-request floor. That gap is exactly the schedulable part (§5): caching fixes the reuse and the compute ceiling, but the p99 coin-flip between the floor and the SLO is a scheduling phenomenon, not a caching one.

5 The lever is scheduling, not caching

Placing the caching result against the other two KV-cache control axes shows the goodput lever is not where the workload's shape suggests:

  • Caching / retention (this paper) — non-lever. LRU is single-pass Belady-optimal; document reuse is already captured; the residual is an un-capturable benchmark artifact; a live LFU/SLRU A/B degrades rather than helps. Zero headroom.
  • Admission (companion result) — non-lever. Deferring expensive prefills cannot help; it worsens the head-of-line blocking below (deferred cold prefills run later and block more turns: device-footprint CCA → λ=3 p99 ~56 s), and cost-blind deferral crashes the server. Zero headroom.
  • Scheduling / reordering — the lever. The λ=3 p99 (6–11 s) is not a per-request floor: fewer than 0.1% of turns prefill ≥6 s, so the tail is head-of-line queue-wait — small turns stuck behind the back-to-back chunked prefill of a few large cold documents. Directly in the stock run, the waiting queue is 9.6× deeper during big-cold-document prefill chunks (mean 11.9, p90 33) than during other prefill steps (mean 1.2, p90 0) (analysis/hol_trace.py) — turns pile up specifically behind the big cold docs. A calibrated prefill-schedule simulation (analysis/hol_sim.py) reproduces the stock coin-flip (p99 mean 7.5 s, straddling the SLO) and shows shortest-prefill-first collapses it to ~4 s with the coin-flip removed (all seeds pass), while round-robin interleaving does not. Above the knee, shortest-prefill-first also raises throughput toward the ~4.2 req/s ceiling (concurrent SRPF work). Scheduling moves the metric at both regimes.

The unifying statement inverts the caching intuition: for long-document conversational serving, the document reuse is corpus-fixed (caching cannot help — the mirage), but the goodput is schedulable — the bottleneck is head-of-line blocking by the heavy cold-document prefills, and prefill scheduling (shortest-prefill-first) is the lever, not cache retention or admission. The residual hard limit is the compute ceiling (~4.2 req/s), movable only compute-side (more prefill FLOPs, or parallelizing a single document's prefill via context parallelism — §5.1). We GPU-confirm scheduling's throughput benefit via the concurrent SRPF result and confirm the λ=3 head-of-line relief in simulation; a same-node GPU A/B of shortest-prefill-first at λ=3 is the natural next validation.

5.1 The compute-side lever (context-parallel prefill) is unavailable in this engine

The natural compute-side attack on the p99 floor is context-parallel (CP) prefill, which splits one long document's prefill across the tensor-parallel ranks to cut its latency. It is well-motivated here: a FLOP breakdown of this model (analysis/prefill_flops.py; 12 full-attention + 36 GatedDeltaNet layers) shows the biggest cold documents — the ones that form the p99 — are increasingly attention-dominated, the O(n²) full-attention rising from ~21% of prefill FLOPs at the median document (18.7k tok) to ~49% at p99 (67k) and ~73% at the max (190.9k). Parallelizing those prefills would therefore cut the tail materially.

But CP is not available for this model in sglang v0.31. Enabling --enable-prefill-cp is a silent no-op: the server initializes with attn_cp_size=1 (the auto-derivation attn_cp_size = tp_size / dp_size is gated to DeepSeek/GLM sparse-attention models via is_deepseek_dsa(), and the hybrid Qwen3_5 model file has no CP code path — only the pure-attention Qwen3-MoE and DSA backends implement it). This is an implementation gap, not a fundamental barrier: CP splits the sequence dimension, so the O(n) per-token work (projections, MoE FFN, linear-attention in/out) parallelizes across ranks directly, attention parallelizes via ring attention, and the GatedDeltaNet recurrence admits a chunked cross-device state scan (an O(cp) serial state-combine on top of intra-rank chunked scans, as in sequence-parallel Mamba) — so prefill would parallelize near-linearly, not be negated by the recurrence. Implementing hybrid-model prefill CP is thus a plausible future lever against the p99 floor; it is simply outside what the current engine and the frozen contract expose. Under this system, then, the p99 floor (largest cold documents, ~5–11 s of prefill) stands: no serving-policy lever moves it, and the one compute-side lever that could is unavailable here.

6 Related work

Prefix/KV caching (SGLang RadixAttention, vLLM prefix caching), hierarchical offload (HiCache, Mooncake, Strata), and eviction theory (LRU/LFU/2Q/GDSF, Belady) all assume re-use exists to capture. Cache-aware scheduling and admission variants likewise presume policy headroom. Our contribution is orthogonal and cautionary: on a realistic long-document workload the re-use is either trivially captured or benchmark-induced, and the metric is corpus-determined. This complements popularity-skew studies (which motivate retention) by identifying the regime — few hot items with short reuse distance plus a singleton tail — in which retention policy provably cannot help.

7 Limitations

The claims are for this fixed workload, model, and two-tier capacity. The document-cache replay abstracts the radix tree at document granularity (faithful because a document is one radix path); the throughput/floor constants are measured with a stated sensitivity range. The eviction/retention findings are for the active UnifiedRadixCache path (full-attention component); a model whose active cache exposed a different eviction interface could behave differently, though the LRU=Belady argument is workload-, not implementation-, specific.
