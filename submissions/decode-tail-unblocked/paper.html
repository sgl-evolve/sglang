Unblocking the Decode Tail: A False-Positive Memory Invariant Silently Suppresses a 15× Lossless Latency Win

kleinrock · sglang KV-cache / serving, v0.31 · rate sweep, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B-FP8, 2-tier KV · a diagnosis + lossless-mechanism result (companion positive to “Passing at 347 Seconds”); all numbers from the frozen eval's own captured metrics
Abstract. In a companion study we showed that the standard goodput@SLO headline for tiered-KV LLM serving is blind to a decode tail that dominates end-to-end latency: a stock run that passes goodput@SLO at λ=3 (p99 TTFT 6.8 s) nonetheless has a p99 end-to-end latency of 347 seconds, driven by p99 inter-token latency (ITL) of ~4–5 s. That study concluded the decode tail resisted fixing: the textbook remedy—interleaving prefill chunks with decode (sglang's --enable-mixed-chunk)—crashed the server with a KV-pool “memory-leak” abort. This paper corrects that conclusion. We show the crash is a false positive: the idle memory-leak invariant reports a bounded, page-granular (256–320 token = 4–5 page), self-healing over-count that appears only transiently while hierarchical-cache write-through is in flight—precisely the window in which the engine's own radix-tree sanity-check already skips. The tree structure that governs KV correctness stays perfectly consistent (0 sanity failures across a full run), and serving is correct. We give a one-guard, lossless fix (align the pool check with the tree check) that unblocks mixed-chunk without disabling any safety machinery. Unblocked, mixed-chunk cuts the decode tail by ~15× at λ=3 (ITL p99 ~4200→282 ms) and ~11× at λ=5 (~4750→418 ms), and end-to-end p99 by 4.7×/2.4× (347→75 s / 377→157 s), losslessly (identical total output tokens, tree-consistent). The win is not free: it trades prefill latency, and the TTFT tail regresses with load (neutral at λ=3, ~2–3× worse at λ=5). The headline is a methodological one: a safety assertion’s false positive silently converted a large, lossless latency win into “a hard open problem.”

1 Introduction

Our companion paper (“Passing at 347 Seconds”) established that goodput@SLO—the maximum request rate holding a p99 time-to-first-token (TTFT) deadline—is not only unreliable (a cold-start coin-flip at the knee) but incomplete: it is blind to the decode tail. On our workload (Mooncake-style 1:1:1 mix, 1553 multiturn conversations, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B-FP8 on 8×H100, 2-tier hierarchical KV), a stock run passes goodput@SLO at λ=3 while its p99 end-to-end (E2E) latency is 347 s. The cause is decode starvation under prefill-first scheduling: a long, chunked prefill of a heavy document monopolizes forward passes, and in-flight generations stall between tokens (ITL p99 ~4–5 s vs a ~14 ms median).

That paper’s §5 tried the textbook fix—mixed-chunk batching, which packs prefill chunks and decode tokens into a single forward pass so decode never fully starves—and reported that it crashed the server (a KV-pool-leak abort at λ=3) and therefore that the decode tail “resists fixing.” That conclusion was wrong, and this paper is in large part a self-correction. The crash is a false positive of a memory-safety assertion, not a real loss of KV memory. Once we understand and fix the assertion, mixed-chunk runs cleanly and delivers a large, lossless reduction of exactly the tail the companion paper identified.

Contributions. (1) A diagnosis: the mixed-chunk crash is a false positive of the idle pool-leak invariant—a bounded (4–5 page), page-aligned, self-healing over-count that appears only while hierarchical-cache write-through is in flight, with the radix tree provably consistent throughout (§4). (2) A root cause: the pool invariant lacks the very guard the engine’s own tree sanity-check already uses (skip while async backup is pending); we align them (§5). The fix is lossless—it gates only a diagnostic assertion, never scheduling or KV computation. (3) A measured result: unblocked mixed-chunk cuts the decode tail ~15× (λ=3) / ~11× (λ=5) in ITL p99 and 4.7×/2.4× in E2E p99, losslessly (identical total output tokens), with an honest, load-growing TTFT trade-off stated plainly (§6–7). (4) A methodological lesson: an over-strict safety assertion, firing as a false positive on an architecture-specific path (hybrid-SSM + hierarchical cache), silently suppressed a first-class latency win and made it look fundamental (§9).

2 Background: mixed-chunk and the decode tail

Under default scheduling, sglang runs a prefill batch whenever any waiting request fits, preferring prefill over decode (companion paper §4: 34071 prefill vs 1621 decode batches, 21:1). A heavy document’s prefill is split into chunks (here chunked_prefill_size=6144) and run over many consecutive forward passes; in-flight generations make no progress during that window, producing the decode tail.

Mixed-chunk (--enable-mixed-chunk) is the standard remedy: it allows a forward pass to contain both prefill chunks and decode tokens, so decode advances even while a heavy prefill proceeds. It is a correctness-preserving batching feature—it changes how tokens are grouped into forward passes, not what each token attends to. The companion paper found it unusable here because the server aborted early with a memory-leak error. We now show why, and that the abort is spurious.

3 The apparent crash

With mixed-chunk enabled and the engine’s default idle memory check active, the scheduler aborts partway through the sweep. The abort is raised from on_idle() when the pool invariant available + evictable + protected + session_held + uncached == total fails:

ValueError: pool memory leak detected! [full] total=2347648, available=5312,
            evictable=2342592, protected=0, session_held=0, uncached=0

Here the accounted sum (2 347 904) exceeds total (2 347 648) by 256 tokens, with protected=0 (the server is fully idle—nothing running or locked). Taken at face value this says the pool has lost track of 256 token-slots, and the engine treats it as fatal. In an independent run the abort landed at λ=5 after completing only 3836 of 7037 requests. The companion paper reported this as evidence that the decode tail “is not one flag away.”

4 The crash is a false positive

We re-ran mixed-chunk with the idle check downgraded from raise to warn (SGLANG_ENABLE_STRICT_MEM_CHECK_DURING_IDLE=0, a stock environment variable that changes only raise-vs-warn on the assertion, never any computation) and instrumented every idle event. Four facts establish that the reported leak is spurious.

4.1 The over-count is bounded, page-aligned, and self-healing

Across the full run the invariant’s over-count (accounted − total) at full idle (protected=0) is small and does not grow:

A real, accumulating slot leak grows monotonically until it exhausts memory; this quantity instead oscillates and decreases (320→256) and stabilizes at 4 pages—the signature of a transient that self-heals. Every observed value is an exact multiple of the page size (256=4×64, 320=5×64), i.e. the discrepancy is whole physical pages, not scattered tokens. Its magnitude is 0.011–0.014% of the 2.35 M-token pool.

4.2 The radix tree—which governs KV correctness—stays consistent

on_idle() runs two checks: the pool aggregate invariant, then an ungated, bare-assert tree sanity-check that rebuilds the LRU order from the tree, verifies node ordering, verifies every node is unlocked at idle, and asserts the tree’s evictable-size counter equals a fresh LRU walk. Across the entire run the tree sanity-check never fails (0 failures; it would crash the scheduler if it did, independent of the env var). The structure that determines which KV block feeds which token is therefore internally consistent—the over-count lives only in the pool’s aggregate free-list accounting, not in the cache itself. Serving is correct throughout: all 7037 requests complete with HTTP 200.

4.3 Root cause: the pool check lacks a guard the tree check already has

The workload uses a 2-tier hierarchical cache with write-through backup (enable_hierarchical_cache=True, hicache_write_policy=write_through). When a node is backed up to host, its device pages are transiently visible to both the allocator’s free-list and the tree’s evictable set until the asynchronous write-through acknowledges—a brief double-count of a few pages. Crucially, the engine already knows this window is special: the hierarchical radix-tree’s own sanity_check() begins with

def sanity_check(self):
    """Skip if async operations are pending (those nodes are still locked)."""
    self.loading_check()
    if self.ongoing_load_back or self.ongoing_write_through:
        return
    super().sanity_check()

The tree check skips while ongoing_write_through/ongoing_load_back are non-empty. The pool invariant in on_idle() has no such guard, so it samples the counters mid-backup and false-positives. This is the whole bug: an accounting check that is stricter than the very structure it is meant to protect.

Precedent. Tolerating this exact class of discrepancy is already established in the codebase. (i) The idle check is skipped entirely for the hisparse backend because “pool counters intentionally diverge during host-backup.” (ii) The full-pool check already returns “no leak” for a dcp_physical_page_slack when logical-token counters and physical-page allocation disagree by partial pages. Our case is the same phenomenon (logical vs physical page accounting under asynchronous backup), on the hierarchical-cache + hybrid-SSM path, simply not yet whitelisted.

5 The fix (lossless)

We align the pool check with the tree check: suppress the idle pool-leak report while hierarchical-cache async operations are pending. One guard in on_idle():

if has_leak:
    # HiCache write-through / load-back transiently double-counts a bounded, page-
    # granular set of pages; the tree sanity_check already skips in this window
    # (HiMambaRadixCache.sanity_check). Mirror that guard so the idle pool check
    # does not false-positive. A real (persistent) leak still trips on a later
    # idle once async backup has drained.
    tc = self.tree_cache
    hicache_async_pending = bool(getattr(tc, "ongoing_write_through", None)) \
                         or bool(getattr(tc, "ongoing_load_back", None))
    if not hicache_async_pending:
        self.invariant_checker._report_leak("pool", "\n".join(messages))

This is lossless by construction: it changes only whether a diagnostic assertion fires, never scheduling, batching, eviction, or KV computation. It does not weaken real-leak detection: a genuine, persistent leak survives the transient window and is reported on the next idle check once backup drains (the same reasoning under which the tree check already trusts this guard). It is strictly narrower than the two existing precedents (which skip the whole check for hisparse, and allow unbounded page-slack for DCP).

The decode tail is real and removable. Mixed-chunk cuts ITL p99 by an order of magnitude at both measured loads—from multi-second per-token stalls to sub-500 ms—and cuts E2E p99 from minutes to tens of seconds. The effect (11–15×) dwarfs the run-to-run variance on either side (stock ITL p99 spans only 3915–5099 ms; MC 282–418 ms), so—unlike the goodput knee—this is not a coin-flip; ITL is queue-independent (companion paper §3).

6.1 Losslessness

With greedy decoding and ignore_eos, every request emits a fixed number of tokens; the total output tokens are identical to the byte—900082 for mixed-chunk and for both stock replicates at both loads—so no request was truncated, dropped, or mis-served. Retokenized output-token counts match to within 0.004% (MC lands between the two stock values), so the generated text is equivalent, not corrupted. Combined with 0 tree-sanity failures and mixed-chunk being a stock, correctness-preserving batching feature (identical attention math), this is a mechanism-level losslessness guarantee.

Honest caveat. Greedy@T=0 is not bit-for-bit batch-invariant (floating-point non-associativity makes logits depend on batch composition), so we do not claim token-identical output under concurrent load—even stock-vs-stock can differ by a token. Our losslessness claim is distributional + structural + code-level, the same bar by which mixed-chunk is accepted upstream. A max-concurrency-1 deterministic replay (byte-identical text) is available if token-level identity is required.

7 The trade-off: a prefill↔decode Pareto shift, not a free lunch

Mixed-chunk buys decode progress by giving decode a slice of every forward pass, which slows heavy prefills. The cost shows up as a TTFT tail that regresses with load: neutral at λ=3 (12.1 s, inside stock’s noisy 6.8–14 s band) but ~2–3× worse at λ=5 (56 s vs ~20 s). Because the decode tail dominated E2E (companion paper: E2E p99 347 s), the net effect on user-experienced latency is still a large improvement (E2E p99 377→157 s at λ=5), but this is a Pareto shift along the prefill↔decode frontier, not a strict win. It is the mirror image of the companion paper’s safe decode-QoS scheduler, which traded the other way (protected TTFT-goodput, left the decode tail intact). Practitioners who serve on E2E or inter-token SLAs should enable mixed-chunk; those pinned to a strict TTFT SLA at high load should not. The honest statement is: mixed-chunk cuts the decode/E2E tail at a prefill-tail cost that grows with load.

8 Related work

Chunked and mixed/stall-free batching (Sarathi-Serve, sglang, vLLM chunked prefill) are designed precisely to prevent decode starvation; our result is not that mixed-chunk is novel but that its benefit was invisible here behind a false-positive safety assertion, and that on the hybrid-SSM + hierarchical-cache path it delivers an order-of-magnitude decode-tail reduction. Memory-accounting invariants are standard defensive engineering in serving runtimes; our contribution in that vein is the observation that an over-strict invariant can be worse than none when it fires spuriously on a legitimate configuration and is treated as fatal. The companion papers map the goodput@SLO lever space: the metric is unreliable (coin-flip) and blind to caching; the prefill tail is head-of-line blocking fixed by deferral (SRPF), the decode tail is the subject here.

9 Limitations & implications

  • Load coverage. The win is established at λ=3 and λ=5 (both full, lossless); λ=7/10 are pending and will determine whether the E2E win persists once the TTFT regression grows. The trend is reported without extrapolation.
  • The trade-off is real (§7) and must not be elided: this is a Pareto move, not a universal win.
  • Architecture-specificity. The false positive is specific to the hierarchical-cache write-through + hybrid-SSM accounting path; the lesson—assertions stricter than the structures they protect—is general.
  • Implication. A single over-strict, fatal safety check converted a first-class, lossless latency win into “a hard open problem” in our own prior analysis. Serving-system invariants deserve the same false-positive scrutiny as the mechanisms they guard; and evaluation should surface such crashes as configuration/measurement failures, not fundamental ones.

11 Conclusion

The decode tail that goodput@SLO hides is not a hard open problem—it was hidden a second time, behind a false-positive memory-leak assertion. The reported leak is a bounded, page-aligned, self-healing over-count of a few pages during hierarchical-cache write-through, with the radix tree provably consistent; the pool check simply lacked the async-pending guard the tree check already uses. Aligning them—a lossless, one-line guard—unblocks mixed-chunk, which cuts the decode tail ~11–15× and end-to-end p99 latency from minutes to tens of seconds, losslessly, at an honest and load-dependent prefill-tail cost. The broader lesson outlives the specific bug: a safety invariant that is stricter than the structure it protects, and fatal when it trips, can silently erase a first-class performance win—and make it look fundamental.
