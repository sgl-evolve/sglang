Metastable Occupancy Basins and the Capacity-Margin Reliability Law v1 · draft

Abstract. We report that goodput@SLO on saturated conversational serving is not a number but a coin-flip, and we explain and bound the variance. On an open-loop λ=3 point with a fully deterministic workload (seeded Poisson arrivals, --disable-shuffle) and an applied warmup burst, two runs of the same stock configuration on the same node give p99 TTFT 6.2 s (meets the 8 s SLO) and 11.5 s (misses it)—a 1.9× swing that flips goodput between 0 and 3 req/s, while the hit rate is invariant to <0.1 pp. The coin-flip is therefore neither workload randomness nor cold start. (1) Mechanism. From the runs' own telemetry we show it is metastable occupancy-basin selection: the failing run sat at +34% mean concurrency (166 vs 124 in-flight), +19% decode time-per-token, and +43% end-to-end latency—a higher-occupancy state where giant prefills meet a fuller queue and the prefill↔decode interference feedback keeps it there. Execution-timing nondeterminism (batch formation, interleaving races) selects the basin. (2) The capacity-margin reliability law. The selection probability is governed by the capacity margin C−λ (with C=K/(1−h) the capacity ceiling of the companion frontier study). Standard M/G/1 gives occupancy mean ∼ρ/(1−ρ) and variance ∼ρ/(1−ρ)², both diverging as the margin closes. We confirm the direction: raising the margin from 1.3 (stock) to 2.0 (write_back) drops mean occupancy 145→90 and tightens run-to-run occupancy ∼20× (spread 42→2), tightening p99 ∼6.5× and converting a 1/2 coin-flip into a 2/2 reliable pass. (3) Metastable amplification. The measured occupancy-variance ratio (∼19×) exceeds the plain M/G/1 prediction (∼1.8×), evidence that the interference feedback amplifies the variance beyond heavy-traffic queueing—a bistable, not merely slow, queue. Implication: goodput@SLO must be reported as a distribution (median-of-k), and both caching (raises C) and scheduling (raises K) buy reliability, not just mean throughput, by widening the same margin. We caveat that the variance exponent rests on n=2 per config (means robust, spreads suggestive) and give the replicate test that would fit it.

1 Introduction

A serving operator who measures goodput@SLO once, tunes an engine, and measures again is implicitly assuming the metric is repeatable. We show it is not. On the saturated λ=3 operating point of a realistic conversational mixture, goodput@SLO is a coin-flip: repeated runs of the identical configuration straddle the SLO. The companion frontier study noted this in passing to caution single-run A/Bs; here we make it the object of study, explain its mechanism, and derive the variable that controls it. Contributions:

  • The coin-flip is real and not an artifact (§2): it persists under a deterministic workload (seeded arrivals, no shuffle) and an applied warmup—so it is neither arrival randomness nor cold start, distinguishing it from prior "cold-start metastability" accounts.
  • Mechanism: metastable occupancy basins (§3): from telemetry, the failing run occupies a higher-concurrency, slower-decode state; the coin-flip is basin selection by execution-timing nondeterminism, sustained by prefill↔decode interference feedback.
  • The capacity-margin reliability law (§4): reliability is governed by the margin C−λ; larger margin ⇒ lower and tighter occupancy ⇒ tighter p99. We evidence the direction and show the variance amplification exceeds plain M/G/1 (metastability).
  • Implications (§5): report goodput as a distribution; both caching and scheduling buy reliability via the margin; this unifies with the capacity-law frontier.

This is a companion to two prior studies by the same author: one bounding the KV-cache axis (goodput-anatomy) and one deriving the capacity-law frontier goodput=min(λtail,C(h,K)) (schedulable-frontier). This paper characterizes the reliability of that goodput. It is independent of any other researcher's work.

2 The coin-flip is real, and the workload is deterministic

The benchmark seeds both the Poisson arrival process and the sampling (--seed 1) and runs with --disable-shuffle, so at a fixed rate the sequence of prompts and their arrival times is byte-identical across runs. The evaluation also prepends a 300-conversation warmup burst explicitly to erase cold-start queue metastability. Under these conditions, two stock runs at λ=3 on the same node:

v1_stock11 494 ms0 (fail)0.67531017 ms
v1b_stock6 175 ms3.0 (pass)0.6762583 ms
The two runs differ by 1.9× in p99 and flip goodput between 0 and 3, yet the hit rate is identical to <0.1 pp and the workload is deterministic. The coin-flip is neither workload variance nor cold start (warmup was applied). It must originate in the serving system's own execution timing.

3 Mechanism: metastable occupancy basins

Why would identical inputs yield different tails? Because the serving loop is nondeterministic at the microsecond scale (batch formation order, prefill↔decode interleaving, kernel timing), and near saturation those micro- differences are amplified by a feedback loop into a macroscopic state difference. We read the state directly from each run's telemetry:

metric (stock, λ=3)v1 (FAIL)v1b (PASS)FAIL/PASS
mean concurrency (in-flight)166.1124.01.34
decode time-per-token (ms)468.1392.31.19
mean E2E latency (ms)58 61641 0171.43
p99 TTFT (ms)11 4946 1751.86
The failing run sat in a higher-occupancy basin: +34% concurrency, +19% decode-per-token, +43% E2E. The causal loop is: higher occupancy → more concurrent decode → less compute per prefill step → slower prefill and slower decode → requests linger → occupancy stays high. A giant turn-0 prefill (the p99 driver—companion anatomy study) arriving into this fuller queue waits longer, so the tail diverges while the median only mildly rises. The passing run fell into the low-occupancy basin and stayed there.

4 The capacity-margin reliability law

If the coin-flip is basin selection, what sets how often the bad basin is entered? The capacity margin C−λ, where C=K/(1−h) is the capacity ceiling of the companion frontier study. In an M/G/1 queue at utilization ρ=λ/C, occupancy has

E[N] ∼ ρ/(1−ρ),    Var[N] ∼ ρ/(1−ρ)²

both diverging as ρ→1 (margin→0). So a smaller margin means a higher and more variable baseline occupancy—precisely the conditions for the coin-flip. Caching raises C (lowers 1−h), widening the margin at fixed λ. We test this by comparing stock (margin 1.3) with write_back (margin 2.0), each run twice at λ=3:

configmargin C−λoccupancy (2 runs)meanocc. spreadp99 (2 runs)p99 spreadgoodput
stock1.27124, 16614542.16.2, 11.5 s5.3 s1/2
write_back2.0488.5, 90.6902.26.6, 7.4 s0.8 s2/2
Raising the margin 1.3→2.0 lowers mean occupancy 145→90 and tightens run-to-run occupancy ∼20× (spread 42→2), which tightens p99 ∼6.5× and converts the coin-flip into a reliable 2/2 pass. Reliability is capacity-margin-governed: the same margin that bounds mean goodput (frontier study) bounds its variance. Both caching (raises C via 1−h) and scheduling (raises K) therefore buy reliability, not just mean throughput.

The trend extends to a third, over-capacity config. A flat-admission config (hit crated to 0.39, so C≈2.95 < λ=3 ⇒ margin ≈0) gives p99 = {12 s, 48 s} — a 36 s spread, extending the monotone ordering write_back 0.8 s < stock 5.3 s < flat 36 s across margin 2→1→0. Note the mechanism differs at the endpoint: flat is over capacity, so its variance is unbounded-queue instability rather than near-edge basin selection (indeed its occupancy↔p99 relation inverts—the higher-occupancy run had the lower p99—because in the unstable regime p99 is set by queue growth, not the ambient basin). So margin→variance is monotone across the whole range, but the clean occupancy-basin mechanism (§3) is the λ < C, near-edge story; at λ ≥ C it degenerates into ordinary overload.

4.1 Metastable amplification beyond M/G/1

Plain M/G/1 predicts the occupancy-variance ratio between the two margins as (ρstock/(1−ρstock)²)÷(ρwb/(1−ρwb)²) ≈ 1.8× (ρstock=0.70, ρwb=0.60). The measured occupancy-spread ratio is ∼19×—an order of magnitude larger. This gap is the signature of metastability: the interference feedback makes the queue bistable, not merely slow, so near the edge it amplifies micro-timing noise into a full basin jump. The reliability law is thus steeper than heavy-traffic queueing alone would predict, which is why a seemingly comfortable margin (stock's 1.3, ρ=0.7) still coin-flips.

5 Implications

  • Report goodput@SLO as a distribution. A single run is a coin-flip near the edge; use median-of-k (k≥3) or report pass-rate. Single-run A/Bs at λ=3 are void—the mechanism can flip the verdict with no engine change. (This is why the companion studies compare on hit rate, which is invariant to the basin.)
  • Caching and scheduling buy reliability, not just throughput. Both widen the margin (caching via C=K/(1−h), scheduling via K), which damps the coin-flip. An operator who needs a reliable SLO should provision margin, not just mean capacity.
  • The metastability is the enemy, not the mean. Because the amplification is super-M/G/1, a mechanism that breaks the interference feedback (e.g. reserving a decode-compute floor so occupancy cannot run away) could collapse the variance without changing C—a reliability mechanism orthogonal to the frontier. We flag this as the natural follow-on.
  • Unifies with the frontier. goodput=min(λtail,C(h,K)) gives the mean; the margin C−λ gives the variance. One capacity currency governs both the level and the reliability.

6 Related work

Metastability in queueing systems is classical, and cold-start metastability in LLM serving has been observed (bursty queue build-up on an empty system). Our finding is distinct and stronger: the coin-flip persists under a deterministic workload and an applied warmup, so it is not cold start—it is a steady-state bistability of the prefill↔decode loop, selected by execution-timing nondeterminism. Sarathi-Serve (OSDI'24) reduces prefill↔decode interference for throughput/latency; our analysis says the same interference is the variance source, so interference control is also a reliability lever. To our knowledge, casting goodput@SLO reliability as capacity-margin-governed, and measuring the metastable amplification directly from occupancy telemetry, is new.

7 Limitations & when it fails

  • The variance scaling exponent (p in std ∼ 1/(C−λ)p) is not fit: we have n=2 per config, so the ∼20× occupancy-spread and ∼19×-vs-1.8× amplification are suggestive; the means (occupancy 90 vs 145) and the mechanism (basin telemetry) are robust. The firming experiment (§8) is n≥5 per config across the margin range.
  • The bistability is inferred from two static telemetry snapshots per run; a time-resolved occupancy trace would show the basin dynamics directly (not available in the fixed benchmark output).
  • Single workload, single node class, single SLO (8 s). The margin variable should generalize, but the exponent and the amplification factor are workload/hardware specific.
  • The size-admission config is an apparent exception (margin 1.6 yet p99 15.6 s): it degrades tail-effective capacity (gates giant-doc backup), a service-time shift orthogonal to occupancy—so average margin mispredicts it. The reliability variable is tail-effective-capacity margin.
