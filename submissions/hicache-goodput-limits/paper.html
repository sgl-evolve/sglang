What Bounds Goodput@SLO in a Two-Tier HiCache under Concurrent Multi-Turn Long-Context Serving?

DRAFT — the empirical core (§5 Evaluation) awaits the frozen rate-sweep baseline (compute-gated). The central claim (a novel residency mechanism vs. a rigorously bounded negative result) is resolved by one measured quantity: the server's prefill throughput P (§4). Abstract. Hierarchical KV caching (HiCache) tiers attention KV across GPU HBM (L1) and host DRAM (L2). We ask a sharp question for a realistic regime — a hybrid Mamba–Transformer MoE (Qwen3.5-122B) serving a Mooncake-style 1:1:1 mix of multi-turn conversations under a Poisson rate sweep, scored by goodput@SLO (max req/s with p99 TTFT ≤ 8 s) — whether the KV cache is the lever for goodput under load, and if so what mechanism captures it losslessly. Through trace-driven analysis of the real workload and the live sglang engine, we establish three facts that sharply constrain the design space: (1) the default scheduler already re-matches every server-side-waiting request each step, refreshing LRU recency, so LRU already protects prefixes of requests visible to the server; (2) L2→L1 load-back is already per-layer overlapped with compute, so speculative prefetch removes only a first-layer bubble; (3) under perfect within-conversation caching, 99% of the irreducible prefill work is cold turn-0 documents, and the p99 of per-turn uncached prefill is large (39K tokens). Consequently, whether p99 TTFT can be reduced by any lossless cache mechanism is decided by the prefill throughput P relative to the SLO: if the long-context cold-prefill tail alone exceeds the SLO for >1% of requests, goodput@SLO is irreducibly bound by context length, not cache efficiency. We ⟨report⟩ the measured regime and either (a) a residency mechanism that captures the ⟨~18.5M-token⟩ avoidable-recompute headroom, or (b) a bounded impossibility result with its regime of validity. Either way the contribution is a precise, generalizable characterization of when the cache is — and is not — the goodput lever in tiered LLM serving.

1 Introduction

Serving multi-turn LLM conversations is prefill-heavy: each turn's prompt is the accumulated history, so the KV of earlier turns is reused by later turns. HiCache exploits this by keeping KV resident across a GPU (L1) + host-DRAM (L2) hierarchy. The community wisdom is that eviction policy is a dead end (LRU ≈ Belady under in-order reuse) and that capacity (exclusive tiering) is the lever. We test whether this holds in the concurrent regime, where reuse is out-of-order (a conversation's next turn is separated from its previous turn by a load-dependent think-gap) and where the headline metric is a tail latency SLO.

Contributions (this draft; empirical core pending):

  • A trace-driven decomposition of the goodput@SLO limiter into (i) irreducible cold-context prefill and (ii) avoidable warm-turn recompute, with a single measurable criterion (prefill throughput P vs. SLO) that decides which dominates (§4).
  • Three engine-level findings that rule out the "obvious" cache mechanisms before any experiment: stock schedule-time matching already protects server-waiting prefixes; load-back is already per-layer overlapped; the residual reuse structure is only predictable by turn-count, i.e. by textbook frequency policies (§3).
  • ⟨Either⟩ a novel lossless residency mechanism that shifts the goodput curve, ⟨or⟩ a rigorously bounded negative result — decided by the measured regime (§5).

2 Motivation (trace-driven)

2.1 Workload

The frozen evaluation serves the real-text Mooncake-style 1:1:1 mix (ShareGPT + LEval + LooGLE) via the loogle multi-turn loader: 1553 conversations, closed-loop within a conversation (turn N+1 is issued only after turn N's response completes) and open-loop Poisson across conversations. Tokenizing the dataset with the model tokenizer:

Workload facts (measured, sim/build_trace.py):
  • 7037 turns; avg 4.53 turns/conv (median 3, max 61); 39.0% of conversations are single-turn.
  • Working set 20.2M tokens = 1.89× the L1+L2 capacity (~10.7M) → genuine eviction pressure.
  • turn-0 documents dominate conversation KV: p50 7.9K, p90 32.6K, max 192.7K tokens.
  • Continuation hazard: P(continue | reached turn 1)=0.61, but 0.75–0.89 for turns ≥2.
  • Within-conversation reuse ceiling = 80.6% of prefill work; top-10% of convs hold 64.6% of reuse value.

2.2 The irreducible cold-context tail

Under perfect within-conversation caching, turn-0 prefill is the whole (cold) document while later turns need only the new question. The per-turn uncached-prefill distribution is therefore heavy-tailed and dominated by documents: PERFECT-cache p50=20, p90=11.4K, p99=39.2K, max=193K tokens; and 99.0% of all irreducible prefill work is turn-0 documents (1.0% is later turns). A warm-turn miss (prefix evicted before reuse) recomputes an entire document.

3 Why the obvious cache mechanisms are already taken (engine analysis)

We read the live engine (UnifiedRadixCache + HybridCacheController + scheduler) for the frozen config (2-tier, page_first_direct/direct, write_through) and find:

  • Stock already protects server-waiting prefixes. Even under the default fcfs policy, calc_priority calls match_prefix_for_req for every waiting request each step (schedule_policy.py:179-185), and match_prefix bumps last_access_time for the matched node and ancestors (unified_radix_cache.py:976-980). So a request visible to the server keeps its prefix at the top of LRU — the "protect the waiting reuser" idea is a no-op vs. stock.
  • Load-back is already per-layer overlapped. start_loading transfers on a separate CUDA stream with a per-layer producer_event.complete(i); the forward waits per-layer via layer_transfer_counter.wait_until(layer_id) (memory_pool.py:1526/1537). Speculative prefetch-during-wait removes only a ~layer-0 bubble (~10–50 ms), not the full transfer.
  • The residual reuse structure is turn-count-predictable only. The vulnerable window for a warm-turn miss is when a conversation's next turn is client-side (concurrency-semaphore-blocked), invisible to the server; the only server-side predictor of continuation is turn-count (=hit_count), i.e. LFU/SLRU/GDSF — textbook policies that prior work reports as neutral/harmful here.

4 The fork criterion (what the baseline decides)

Let P be the server's effective prefill throughput (tokens/s). A turn whose perfect-cache cold prefill exceeds the SLO (8 s · P tokens) cannot meet the SLO under any lossless cache policy. Applying the measured per-turn distribution:

If mechanism-viable, the target is the avoidable-recompute headroom: baseline uncached ⟨~37.8M⟩ tok (hit 0.62) vs. perfect ⟨~19.3M⟩ → ⟨~18.5M⟩ tok (~49% of baseline prefill) of warm-turn recompute that a better-than-LRU finite-cache policy could remove. We measure P from the baseline (server-log input throughput + curve.csv p99-vs-λ + hit-rate-vs-λ) and resolve the fork.

6 Related work

Tiered KV caching & prefetch. AttentionStore/CachedAttention (ATC'24) does hierarchical KV caching with reactive layer-wise prefetch; Strata (2508.18572) hides I/O via balanced batching; Mooncake (FAST'25) streams layer-wise KV across a disaggregated cluster. Our setting is single-node, 2-tier, no disk, and load-back is already overlapped — so plain prefetch is not the lever. Reuse & scheduling. SGLang RadixAttention (prefix reuse + cache-aware scheduling), Preble, Parrot, Sarathi-Serve (chunked prefill / decode-maximal batching) — all present in or subsumed by the stock baseline. Cost/predictive residency. AsymCache, Predictive-Multi-Tier, Continuum (TTL-aware) price recompute or predict reuse; these overlap the turn-count-predictable residual we identify. Disaggregated append-prefill. PPD (2603.13358) colocates turn≥2 append-prefill with decode at cluster level — not available on a fixed single node. We position our contribution as either the first mechanism to capture the concurrent-regime out-of-order-reuse headroom on a single-node 2-tier cache, or a bounded negative delimiting when that headroom is real. expand w/ full citations
