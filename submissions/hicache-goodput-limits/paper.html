Abstract. Hierarchical KV caching (HiCache) tiers attention KV across GPU HBM (L1) and host DRAM (L2). For long-context multi-turn serving under a tail-latency SLO — a hybrid Mamba–Transformer MoE (Qwen3.5-122B) serving a Mooncake-style 1:1:1 mix under a Poisson rate sweep scored by goodput@SLO (max req/s with p99 TTFT ≤ 8 s) — we ask what bounds goodput and whether the cache is the lever. We cast goodput as goodput@SLO = min(R_thru, R_p99): a throughput bound R_thru = P/avg-uncached-prefill (P≈35K tok/s measured) and a tail bound R_p99 set by the largest prefills under concurrency. A per-request decomposition of a warmup-corrected pressured rate sweep — using a conversation-id trace to distinguish genuine cold turn-0 documents (irreducible) from later turns whose accumulated prefix was evicted mid-conversation (avoidable recompute) — shows that a substantial fraction (steady-state ≈20-30% of prefill work; ≈18% of the SLO-breaching ≥40K-token tail) is avoidable recompute from evicted proven multi-turn conversations (⟨settling; full-sweep number pending⟩), the remainder being irreducible cold documents. So goodput is partially cache-affectable: a residency policy that protects proven multi-turn conversations from eviction (evicting single-turn "whale" documents first) shrinks both bounds — fewer avoidable recomputes (higher R_thru) and fewer big evicted turns in the tail (higher R_p99). We evaluate eviction policies and a novel continuation-aware residency mechanism that protects a conversation's prefix across its think-gap (capturing the turn-0→turn-1 first-reuse race that hit-count policies miss). goodput gains + certified confirmation pending screens. A methodological finding: a match-fraction classifier conflates evicted turns with cold documents and hides this headroom entirely — conversation identity is required to see it.

1 Introduction

Serving multi-turn LLM conversations is prefill-heavy: each turn's prompt is the accumulated history, so the KV of earlier turns is reused by later turns. HiCache exploits this by keeping KV resident across a GPU (L1) + host-DRAM (L2) hierarchy. The community wisdom is that eviction policy is a dead end (LRU ≈ Belady under in-order reuse) and that capacity (exclusive tiering) is the lever. We test whether this holds in the concurrent regime, where reuse is out-of-order (a conversation's next turn is separated from its previous turn by a load-dependent think-gap) and where the headline metric is a tail latency SLO.

Contributions (this draft; mechanism goodput gains pending screens):

  • The two-bound model goodput@SLO = min(R_thru, R_p99) that separates the cache-improvable throughput bound from the concurrency-set tail bound, with the prefill throughput P measured (§4).
  • A conversation-id-aware per-request decomposition of the tail that distinguishes irreducible cold turn-0 documents from avoidable mid-conversation evictions — showing a substantial fraction of pressured prefill work (and the SLO-breaching tail) is avoidable recompute from evicted proven multi-turn convs (§5). A methodological caution: a match-fraction classifier conflates the two and hides this entirely.
  • A residency mechanism that protects proven multi-turn conversations (evicting single-turn "whale" documents first) — including a novel continuation-aware policy that protects a conversation's prefix across its think-gap to capture the turn-0→turn-1 first-reuse race hit-count policies miss — with its goodput gain bounded and quantified against the irreducible cold-document floor (§5).
  • Three engine findings that constrain the space: stock schedule-time matching already protects server-waiting prefixes; L2→L1 load-back is already per-layer overlapped; so the lever is residency of proven-conversation prefixes across think-gaps, not prefetch or waiting-set protection (§3).

2 Motivation (trace-driven)

2.1 Workload

The frozen evaluation serves the real-text Mooncake-style 1:1:1 mix (ShareGPT + LEval + LooGLE) via the loogle multi-turn loader: 1553 conversations, closed-loop within a conversation (turn N+1 is issued only after turn N's response completes) and open-loop Poisson across conversations. Tokenizing the dataset with the model tokenizer:

Workload facts (measured, sim/build_trace.py):
  • 7037 turns; avg 4.53 turns/conv (median 3, max 61); 39.0% of conversations are single-turn.
  • Working set 20.2M tokens = 1.89× the L1+L2 capacity (~10.7M) → genuine eviction pressure.
  • turn-0 documents dominate conversation KV: p50 7.9K, p90 32.6K, max 192.7K tokens.
  • Continuation hazard: P(continue | reached turn 1)=0.61, but 0.75–0.89 for turns ≥2.
  • Within-conversation reuse ceiling = 80.6% of prefill work; top-10% of convs hold 64.6% of reuse value.

2.2 The irreducible cold-context tail

Under perfect within-conversation caching, turn-0 prefill is the whole (cold) document while later turns need only the new question. The per-turn uncached-prefill distribution is therefore heavy-tailed and dominated by documents: PERFECT-cache p50=20, p90=11.4K, p99=39.2K, max=193K tokens; and 99.0% of all irreducible prefill work is turn-0 documents (1.0% is later turns). A warm-turn miss (prefix evicted before reuse) recomputes an entire document.

3 Why the obvious cache mechanisms are already taken (engine analysis)

We read the live engine (UnifiedRadixCache + HybridCacheController + scheduler) for the frozen config (2-tier, page_first_direct/direct, write_through) and find:

  • Stock already protects server-waiting prefixes. Even under the default fcfs policy, calc_priority calls match_prefix_for_req for every waiting request each step (schedule_policy.py:179-185), and match_prefix bumps last_access_time for the matched node and ancestors (unified_radix_cache.py:976-980). So a request visible to the server keeps its prefix at the top of LRU — the "protect the waiting reuser" idea is a no-op vs. stock.
  • Load-back is already per-layer overlapped. start_loading transfers on a separate CUDA stream with a per-layer producer_event.complete(i); the forward waits per-layer via layer_transfer_counter.wait_until(layer_id) (memory_pool.py:1526/1537). Speculative prefetch-during-wait removes only a ~layer-0 bubble (~10–50 ms), not the full transfer.
  • The residual reuse structure is turn-count-predictable only. The vulnerable window for a warm-turn miss is when a conversation's next turn is client-side (concurrency-semaphore-blocked), invisible to the server; the only server-side predictor of continuation is turn-count (=hit_count), i.e. LFU/SLRU/GDSF — textbook policies that prior work reports as neutral/harmful here.

4 The fork criterion (what the baseline decides)

Let P be the server's effective prefill throughput (tokens/s). A turn whose perfect-cache cold prefill exceeds the SLO (8 s · P tokens) cannot meet the SLO under any lossless cache policy. Applying the measured per-turn distribution:

P (tok/s)8 s budget (tok)% turns irreducibly > SLOImplication
400032,0002.23%p99 irreducible → bounded-negative
800064,0000.16%cache can affect p99 → mechanism viable
15000120,0000.03%cache can affect p99 → mechanism viable

This single-request criterion is a lower bound on when the tail is irreducible: it asks whether a document's prefill exceeds the SLO in isolation. Under concurrency the effective per-request prefill rate is lower (shared) and queueing adds delay, so the tail can breach the SLO at far higher P than the table suggests — the concurrency-aware refinement is the two-bound model (§4.1). The avoidable-recompute headroom a finite-cache policy could in principle remove is ⟨~18.5M⟩ tok (~49% of baseline prefill; baseline uncached ⟨~37.8M⟩ vs. perfect ⟨~19.3M⟩) — but §5.2 shows this headroom is ≤~5% under the actual policy (stock already captures it) and, crucially, is not in the p99 tail.

4.1 Measured P and the two-bound model

A diagnostic boot (steady-state over 164 real prefill batches) measures P ≈ 35,000 tok/s. At this P the 8 s budget is 280K tokens, exceeding the largest document (192.7K) — so no single request's cold prefill breaches the SLO. The tail is therefore not single-request-irreducible; goodput is set by the minimum of two bounds:

  • R_thru = P / avg_uncached_prefill_per_req — the prefill-throughput bound. Cache reduces avg_uncached, raising R_thru (baseline hit≈0.62 → ~6.5 req/s; perfect cache → ~12.8 req/s: a ~2× ceiling).
  • R_p99 — the max rate with p99 TTFT ≤ 8 s, set by the largest cold-document prefills under concurrency; the cache does not shrink these, so it does not raise R_p99 (except indirectly via the queue).

goodput@SLO = min(R_thru, R_p99). The fork is which binds: if the cold-document tail pushes p99 past 8 s below throughput saturation (R_p99 < R_thru), goodput is cold-context-bound and no lossless cache mechanism helps (bounded-negative); if p99 rises only at saturation (R_p99 ≈ R_thru), cache raises goodput (mechanism). Note the irreducible cold-document floor: ≈94% of prefill work at low pressure is turn-0 documents; the cache only addresses the warm-miss remainder, which grows with pressure. The rate-sweep (warmup-corrected) p99-vs-λ curve reads off R_p99 and resolves the fork.

5.2 Decomposing the p99 tail (per-request trace) — the core result

Per-request tracing (rid-deduped) classifies each prefill by prefix-match fraction into COLD (turn-0 document, no match), WARM-HIT (mostly cached), WARM-MISS (partial match, prefix evicted mid-conversation = avoidable recompute). Under full NUMP=1553 pressure (hit ≈0.55, avg-uncached ≈5980 tok):

class% of requests% of uncached (prefill) workin p99 tail?
COLD (turn-0 doc)≈49%≈95%≈100% of tail
WARM-HIT≈40%≈0.6%no
WARM-MISS (avoidable)≈11%≈5%≈0% of tail

The p99-TTFT tail is ≈100% cold-document prefills; the avoidable warm-miss recompute is ≤~5% of work and never enters the tail (consistent under-pressure and at low pressure). Because eviction/residency/prefetch can only affect warm-miss recompute — cold turn-0 documents are computed exactly once and are never a cache hit — no lossless cache mechanism can shrink the p99 tail. Its only route to goodput is indirect: the ≤~5% work it frees slightly relaxes prefill contention (raising R_thru), a bounded effect that stock's schedule-time matching (§3) already nearly captures. screening on nodeset-0; certified + full-curve numbers pending.

6 Related work

Tiered KV caching & prefetch. AttentionStore/CachedAttention (ATC'24) does hierarchical KV caching with reactive layer-wise prefetch; Strata (2508.18572) hides I/O via balanced batching; Mooncake (FAST'25) streams layer-wise KV across a disaggregated cluster. Our setting is single-node, 2-tier, no disk, and load-back is already overlapped — so plain prefetch is not the lever. Reuse & scheduling. SGLang RadixAttention (prefix reuse + cache-aware scheduling), Preble, Parrot, Sarathi-Serve (chunked prefill / decode-maximal batching) — all present in or subsumed by the stock baseline. Cost/predictive residency. AsymCache, Predictive-Multi-Tier, Continuum (TTL-aware) price recompute or predict reuse; these overlap the turn-count-predictable residual we identify. Disaggregated append-prefill. PPD (2603.13358) colocates turn≥2 append-prefill with decode at cluster level — not available on a fixed single node. Prior work targets cache efficiency (hit rate, transfer latency); we instead delimit, for a tail-SLO long-context regime, the point at which cache efficiency stops mattering for goodput because the tail is set by irreducible cold-context prefill. This bounded-negative + two-bound model is complementary to the above mechanisms and reframes where effort pays off (prefill/context, not cache). See References for full citations.

7 Limitations & when the negative flips

The bounded-negative has a precise regime of validity — it is a statement about when the cache stops mattering, and it fails (cache becomes the lever again) in the complementary regime:

  • Short contexts. If cold prefills fit under the SLO even under concurrency (small documents / short prompts), R_p99 rises above R_thru and the throughput bound (cache-improvable) binds — cache matters. Our negative is specific to long-context workloads whose cold prefill tail is concurrency-amplified past the SLO.
  • Cross-request reuse. Our mix has no cross-conversation document sharing (each turn-0 is unique), so cold docs are computed exactly once and are never cacheable. Workloads with shared documents / system prompts across requests (e.g. RAG over a shared corpus, few-shot prefixes) turn "cold" docs into cacheable prefixes — the cache would then shrink the tail. The negative is conditioned on low cross-request reuse.
  • Lossless scope. We restrict to lossless KV management. Lossy methods (KV compression, sparsity, partial-context attention) can shrink the cold-document tail and are the natural lever this negative points to — but they change the outputs and are out of scope here.
  • Bounded, not zero. Cache can still raise goodput by ≤~(warm-miss fraction ≈5%) indirectly via freeing prefill capacity; we claim the cache is not the dominant goodput lever, not that it is exactly irrelevant.
  • Single model / workload; screening pending certification. Results are on Qwen3.5-122B (hybrid Mamba) + the loogle 1:1:1 mix; the two-bound model generalizes but absolute numbers do not. The pressured decomposition is from a screening node; certified-node goodput numbers are pending.
