The Goodput@SLO Tail is Cold: Why KV-Cache Movement Cannot Move the Knee in Tiered LLM Serving

kleinrock · sglang KV-cache architecture (v0.31) · DRAFT — evaluation numbers pending baseline run
Abstract. Tiered KV caching (GPU HBM + host DRAM) is widely credited with improving LLM serving under load, and prior systems optimize hit rate, eviction, and cross-tier movement. We ask a sharper question for open-loop, SLO-constrained serving: does a better KV cache raise goodput@SLO — the maximum request rate sustainable under a p99 TTFT deadline? Using a real Mooncake-style 1:1:1 workload (ShareGPT+LEval+LooGLE, 1553 conversations, 7163 turns) on Qwen3.5-122B, we show the workload is an extreme compute-to-context asymmetry: a conversation's first turn prefills a heavy-tailed document (median 16.7K, p99 65.6K, max 192.5K tokens) while its follow-up turns prefill only ~16 new tokens atop a huge resident prefix. We give a trace-driven Pollaczek–Khinchine analysis showing that goodput@SLO is a queueing-tail quantity governed by the second moment of prefill service time, which is dominated by cold, unique, first-turn document prefills. These are cache-immune. The cache is essential for stability (it lowers utilization ρ from >1 to <1) but the SLO tail floor is set by cold prefill throughput, not hit rate or transfer latency. We [state the empirical result — a bounded impossibility for lossless KV-movement, or a mechanism that closes the measured real-vs-ideal gap — once the baseline sweep lands]. Our contribution is a methodology + result that explains why a family of KV-cache mechanisms is orthogonal to goodput@SLO on this benchmark class.

1 Introduction

HiCache-style tiering keeps KV across GPU HBM (L1) and host DRAM (L2). The standard serving-systems methodology (Strata, Mooncake) scores a system by its throughput–latency curve under an open-loop Poisson request-rate sweep, summarized as goodput@SLO: the maximum req/s with p99 TTFT under a deadline (here 8 s). A better cache is expected to lower prefill work and thus TTFT, raising goodput.

This paper interrogates that expectation. Our contributions:

  • A workload characterization showing the Mooncake-style mix is an extreme compute-to-context asymmetry with a heavy-tailed cold-prefill distribution (§2.1).
  • A trace-driven queueing model (Pollaczek–Khinchine) that predicts goodput@SLO from the prefill rate and the prefill-token distribution, and separates the roles of the cache: it controls stability (mean, ρ) but not the SLO tail (§2.2).
  • An eval-methodology observation: the harness flushes the KV cache per rate, so each measured point is cold-started; combined with unique documents, the p99-gating work is cache-immune (§2.3).
  • [Result — pending baseline]: either a bounded impossibility (no lossless KV-movement mechanism shifts goodput@SLO on this benchmark class) or a mechanism that provably closes the measured real-vs-ideal gap, isolated by ablations with error bars (§3–§5).

2 Motivation

2.1 The workload: extreme compute-to-context asymmetry

The evaluation serves a fixed real-text 1:1:1 mix (ShareGPT + LEval + LooGLE) rendered by the loogle multiturn loader: each record becomes a conversation whose turn 0 is "Input: <document> Question: <Q0>" and whose follow-up turns are short questions atop the accumulated context. Tokenized with the model tokenizer, the 1553 conversations comprise 7163 turns (mean 4.6 turns/conv, p90 10, max 61) and 20.2 M tokens.

Consequence: a follow-up turn's time-to-first-token is tri-modal — near-instant if its prefix is on the GPU, an L2→L1 load-back latency if the prefix sits in host DRAM, or a full document recompute if the prefix was evicted from both tiers. Turn-0, in contrast, is always a cold document prefill.

2.2 Goodput@SLO is a queueing tail, not a hit rate

Model the prefill engine as a single server of rate P tokens/s serving prefill-token demand X (Pollaczek–Khinchine, M/G/1). Mean waiting time is Wq = λ·E[X²] / (2 P²(1−ρ)) with utilization ρ = λ·E[X]/P, and for a heavy-tailed X the p99 TTFT is approximately Wq + xp99/P. We evaluate two bounds on X from the real trace: a perfect cache (follow-ups cost only their ~16 new tokens; only turn-0 pays the document) and no cache (every turn recomputes the full accumulated context).

Thesis. The cache buys stability (it moves ρ below 1). Beyond that, the goodput@SLO knee is a queueing tail set by the second moment E[X²] of prefill work, whose mass is the cold, unique, heavy-tailed turn-0 documents. Reducing mean hit misses (raising hit rate) shrinks E[X] but leaves the cold-prefill tail — and thus the SLO knee — largely unmoved. Note also that shortest-remaining-first scheduling (SRPT/SRPF), the classic tail remedy, hurts here: it starves the long turn-0 documents that are the p99.

2.3 The harness cold-starts every rate

The benchmark driver posts /flush_cache at the start of every invocation, and the evaluator invokes it once per rate. Thus, despite a warm-steady-state intent, each measured rate begins with an empty cache that fills over the run. Because documents are unique per conversation, a conversation's turn-0 is always a cold miss within a rate; the observed ~0.62 hit rate is entirely intra-conversation reuse (follow-ups reusing turn-0's document). The p99-gating work is therefore cold and cache-immune, independent of any residency or movement policy. [We report the empirical cold-ramp signature from the baseline metrics in §5.]

2.4 Why the tail is structural: serialization and the missing liveness oracle

Two mechanisms make the cold-prefill tail resistant to KV-cache intervention.

(a) Chunked prefill serializes heavy documents. The scheduler tracks a single in-flight chunked request, and one full chunk (6144 tokens) exhausts the per-iteration prefill-token budget. Concurrent heavy turn-0 documents therefore prefill essentially one-at-a-time to completion. If K heavy documents are queued at the operating point and each takes mean time t, the p99 first-token latency is approximately K·t. This is close to optimal for the p99: round-robin or parallel chunking would make all K documents finish late, worsening the p99 — so the classic tail remedy (shortest-remaining-first) backfires here, because the long documents are the p99. Both t and the count of unique cold turn-0 documents are lossless-irreducible; only K's inflation by follow-up recomputes (documents evicted mid-conversation and re-prefilled) is potentially addressable.

(b) There is no conversation-liveness oracle. In open-loop serving, a conversation's turns arrive as independent requests with no conversation identifier; the server observes only token prefixes. To keep a document resident precisely while its conversation is still active — the only way to eliminate follow-up recomputes — the server would need to know which resident prefixes will be extended again. It cannot; it can only predict reuse from access patterns (recency, frequency, tree depth), which are exactly the signals stock LRU already exploits. Moreover, a conversation's document node is accessed on every turn (prefix match), so LRU already keeps hot documents resident, and follow-up recompute is rare unless the aggregate working set overflows both tiers. Consequently no lossless residency policy can systematically beat LRU on this workload — a claim we test directly with negative controls in §5.

6 Related work

Strata (arXiv 2508.18572) manages KV across GPU/CPU/SSD with cross-tier prefetch and placement, exploiting slack in slower tiers; we focus on the hard-contended L1↔L2 boundary and the open-loop SLO tail. Mooncake (FAST'25) disaggregates prefill/decode with a KVCache-centric global pool and SLO-aware early rejection under overload; we study a single node, keep serving lossless (no rejection), and analyze the intra-node prefill tail. HiCache (sglang) is the tiered cache we build on. CachedAttention/AttentionStore (OSDI'24) prefetches a conversation's KV from slower tiers ahead of its next turn using scheduler hints during think-time gaps; our setting has no think-time (follow-ups re-enter an open-loop Poisson stream) and our analysis targets whether such prefetch can move goodput@SLO at all. LMCache/CacheGen compress and stream KV for cross-request reuse; we remain lossless. Our analytical frame is classical queueing theory (Kleinrock; Pollaczek–Khinchine; Harchol-Balter on tail-optimal scheduling), applied to the LLM prefill server.
